Foundations

How synthetic aperture radar images the Earth

A SAR sends microwave pulses sideways from a moving platform and records the echoes. It brings its own illumination and uses wavelengths that pass through cloud, so it images day and night in any weather.

Core ideas

Real vs synthetic aperture
A real antenna resolves azimuth at ~R·λ/L (kilometres from orbit). A SAR combines echoes along-track as if a very long antenna had been used.
Range resolution = c / 2B
Set by transmitted bandwidth. A linear FM chirp + matched filter compresses a long pulse into a short, sharp one.
Azimuth resolution ≈ L / 2
For stripmap mode it does not depend on range or wavelength. A shorter antenna gives a wider beam and longer synthetic aperture.
Doppler history
As the platform passes a target, the echo frequency sweeps through a Doppler chirp. Azimuth compression exploits this phase history.
PRF window
PRF must exceed the Doppler bandwidth (~2v/L) and stay below the range-ambiguity limit set by the slant-range swath.
Speckle
Scatterers inside one resolution cell interfere coherently → grainy texture. Multilooking averages it down at the cost of resolution.
Polarimetry
HH, HV, VH, VV respond differently to rough surfaces, volume scattering (vegetation) and double-bounce (buildings).
Interferometry
Phase difference between two passes measures line-of-sight motion. One fringe = half a wavelength of displacement.

Mission field guide

NISAR: two radars, one observatory

The NASA–ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar mission combines L-band and S-band observations to study changes across Earth’s land and ice surfaces.

NASA · L-BAND

~24 cm wavelength

Best for forest structure, biomass, soil moisture and surface deformation. Longer wavelengths penetrate further into vegetation.

Coverage
Global
Archive
ASF DAAC / Earthdata
Public release
20 July 2026 (provisional)
ISRO · S-BAND

~9.4 cm wavelength

Adds sensitivity to crops and finer vegetation, supporting science and applications important to India.

Coverage
India + global sites
Archive
Bhoonidhi / NRSC
Operational
Cycle 25 · 8 July 2026
Instrument

12 m mesh reflector

Large deployable antenna shared by both radars. Fully deployed 17 days after launch.

Mode

SweepSAR

Digital beamforming on receive yields ~242 km swath at full resolution — solving the classic swath–resolution trade-off.

Orbit

747 km · 12-day repeat

Sun-synchronous, inclination 98.4°. Ascending and descending passes over the same ground track.

Mission timeline

  1. 30 JUL 2025
    Launch on GSLV-F16

    Lift-off from Sriharikota; target orbit ~747 km, sun-synchronous.

  2. +17 DAYS
    Reflector fully deployed

    Staged hinge sequence completed successfully.

  3. 23 JAN 2026
    First L-band sample products

    Level 1–3 samples for users to test access and metadata.

  4. 27 FEB 2026
    L-band BETA release

    >100 000 products via ASF DAAC; early products not fully calibrated.

  5. 8 JUL 2026
    S-band Cycle 25 operations

    Operational production from Cycle 25; baseline v1.4.0 (CRID P00500).

  6. 20 JUL 2026
    L-band PROVISIONAL release

    Calibrated products from 17 June 2026 onward (CRID P05023).

  7. NOV 2026
    First VALIDATED collections

    Validated L-band collections; SME2 soil-moisture products remained provisional.

Note. Release dates and processing baselines change. Always confirm current status on official NASA/ASF DAAC and ISRO/Bhoonidhi pages.
